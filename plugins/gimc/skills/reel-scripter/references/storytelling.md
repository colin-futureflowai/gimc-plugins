# Storytelling for short video — the nine principles

The one place these principles live. `reel-scripter` writes and reviews with them, `scripts/script-check.mjs`
measures the parts that can be counted, and other skills point here instead of repeating them.

Each principle: **why** it matters, **how** to apply it, **how you spot it missing**, and a before → after in Dutch
and English. The examples are made up; never reuse them as a client's words or numbers.

The spine of all nine is the **question chain** (last section): every line answers the question the previous line
raised and raises the next one.

---

## 1. Curiosity through contrast

**Why.** People keep watching while a question in their head is still open. The strongest questions come from a
clash between what the viewer already believes and what you show them. No clash, no question; no question, no
reason to stay.

**How.**
1. Pick a pain the viewer really feels (not a topic you find interesting).
2. Write down what most of them believe about it — the usual advice, the obvious fix.
3. Write your opposite view, one you actually hold and can back up.
4. Say why your view solves the pain better.
5. Give one quick proof: a result, a number of your own, or an explanation they recognise at once.

A bolder claim buys less patience: the further it is from what they believe, the sooner the proof has to come.
Viewers who care a lot and are unsure of their current belief give you the most time. Aim for at least one big
contrast moment per script, and more small "huh, I didn't know that" lines along the way.

**Missing when** the opening agrees with what everyone already thinks, or the script explains a topic without ever
saying what is usually believed about it.

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| NL: "Vandaag vertel ik iets over koud douchen." | "Koud douchen maakt je niet wakker. Het maakt je moe — tenzij je het op dit moment doet." |
| EN: "Let's talk about to-do lists." | "Your to-do list is why you finish nothing. Mine has three lines, and that's the point." |

## 2. Speed to value

**Why.** From the first frame the viewer is deciding whether to stay. Give them a reason within about **5 seconds**:
a sign that something worth hearing is coming. The sharper the pain, the more time you get, but plan for 5.

**What counts as value.** In entertainment: something interesting or surprising. In education: a non-obvious fix the
viewer can use. You don't have to deliver it in 5 seconds; you have to *signal* it: a promise, a claim, a number, a
result, "here's how".

**How.** Cut every warm-up: greetings, "in this video", context the viewer doesn't need yet. Put the promise or the
contrast in the first sentence. In a script, tag that sentence `[value]` so script-check can time it.

**Missing when** the first 5 seconds are a name, a greeting, background or a question with no stakes.

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| NL: "Hoi! Ik ben Sanne en ik werk al tien jaar als boekhouder, en vandaag…" | "Drie bonnetjes die je nu weggooit, zijn samen € 400 aftrek waard." |
| EN: "So a lot of people have been asking me about sleep lately…" | "You don't need eight hours. You need the same wake-up time, seven days a week." |

## 3. Value density

**Why.** Speed to value is about the start; density is about every second after it. The more of the script is
useful or surprising, the longer people stay. Every line the viewer doesn't need is a moment to leave.

**How.** For each line ask: would the viewer miss it? If not, cut it. In an educational reel, test the end: can the
viewer *do* something after watching — a step, a sentence to use, a setting to change? Name that action plainly.
In a story, count how often something unexpected happens; long stretches without a turn are where people drop.

**Missing when** the script repeats its setup, tells you what it is going to tell you, or ends without anything the
viewer can act on.

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| NL: "Er zijn heel veel manieren om beter te plannen, en iedereen doet het anders, maar wat ik wil zeggen is dat…" | "Plan morgen vandaag, om vier uur. Drie taken. Niet meer." |
| EN: "There's a lot to say about cold email, and it's a big topic, but basically…" | "Cut your cold email to four lines. Line one: their problem. Not your company." |

## 4. Clarity

**Why.** Nothing transfers if it isn't understood, and on a phone it has to be understood at full speed, once.

**How.**
- **Plain words.** Dutch: B1 level — everyday words, short words, few compounds; a word of five or more syllables
  is a warning sign. English: about grade 6–8. A term or name is fine now and then, if you say right away what it
  means in plain words.
- **Active voice.** Who does what. "The team made the plan", not "the plan was made". Shorter and easier to follow.
- **Say the key point twice.** Once short (with the term if you need it), once more in simple words or with an
  example. The second time is for everyone who missed the first.
- **Compress.** Reduce each point to the smallest form that still carries it. Every extra word is one more thing
  the listener has to hold and throw away.
- **Reframe simpler.** Describe what something *does* for the viewer, not what it *is* in the abstract.

**Missing when** you need to rewind to follow a sentence, a term goes unexplained, or the core point is said once.

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| NL: "Door middel van automatisering kan de efficiëntie van je klantcommunicatie worden geoptimaliseerd." | "Laat je mailbox zelf de standaardvragen beantwoorden. Jij doet alleen nog de lastige." |
| EN: "Retrieval-augmented generation enables contextual grounding of model outputs." | "RAG means the AI reads your documents before it answers. So it quotes you, not the internet." |

## 5. Absorption: easy to take in, easy to do

**Why.** People trust you when your advice works for them, and it only works if they understood it *and* the step
to doing it is small. So make it easy to take in and short to act on.

**How.** Explain a new idea with something they already know: a comparison, a picture, a small framework with a
name. Then give the first step in a form they can do today. A list of three beats a list of nine.

**Missing when** the advice is right but abstract ("be more consistent"), or the first step needs tools, time or
knowledge the viewer doesn't have.

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| NL: "Je moet je klanten beter segmenteren." | "Zet je klanten in drie bakjes: kopen nu, kopen later, kopen nooit. Bel vandaag alleen bakje één." |
| EN: "Be more consistent with content." | "Film four videos every Sunday. One sitting, four outfits. Your week is done." |

## 6. Anticipation

**Why.** If viewers can feel that something good is coming — and roughly when — they wait for it. Anticipation that
ends in a surprising reveal is the strongest hold you have.

**How.**
1. **Tease the payoff early without giving it.** Say that it is coming and where: "the last one is the one that
   worked", "step four is where it goes wrong".
2. **Show the road.** Number or date the beats (1 → 4, day 1 → day 6) so the viewer knows how far the payoff is.
3. **Raise the stakes each beat** and keep the best moment for the end.
4. **Show the result, hold back the how.** The outcome up front makes them stay for the method.
5. **Make the reward concrete:** the exact result, the template, the number. "Stay tuned" holds nobody.
6. **Pay it off, for real.** Every question you open, you close. A teased payoff that never comes feels like a trick
   and costs saves, shares and trust. Never resolve the main question early.
7. **Re-hook where attention dips** (around 4, 9 and 15 seconds) with a short new open question.

**Missing when** the payoff is given away in the middle, the beats don't build, or the end doesn't deliver what
the opening promised.

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| NL: "Hier zijn vier tips voor betere foto's." | "Vier tips voor betere foto's. De vierde kost niks en doet het meeste." |
| EN: "I tried posting every day. Here's what happened." | "I posted every day for 30 days. Day 21 changed how I write every caption." |

## 7. Emotional resonance

**Why.** People act on what they feel. A script that makes them feel something gets comments, saves and shares;
a neutral one gets a nod and a scroll. Shock and fear travel furthest; joy, surprise and envy work too.

**How.** Choose the one emotion you want the viewer to feel *before* you write. Then pick words, images and sounds
that carry that emotion, and cut the ones that work against it. Concrete beats abstract: "your Monday inbox" feels
more than "your workload".

**Missing when** you can't name the emotion the script is going for, or every line is a calm statement of fact.

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| NL: "Veel ondernemers verliezen omzet door trage opvolging." | "Elke avond ligt er een klant in je DM die jij pas morgen ziet. Dan heeft hij al gekocht. Bij een ander." |
| EN: "Backups are important for small businesses." | "One spilled coffee. Three years of client files. Gone by lunch." |

## 8. Rhythm

**Why.** Sentences of the same length in a row make a drone the ear tunes out. A listener also holds only so much:
long chains of clauses push out what came first. Short sentences land, give the brain a beat to store them, and
make room for the next.

**How.** Write mostly short sentences (about eight words or fewer), with now and then a medium or a longer one for
contrast. Vary on purpose: short, short, longer, short. Never trade clarity for rhythm. Read it out loud: where you
run out of breath, split.

**Missing when** three or more sentences in a row have the same length, or sentences run past 18 words.

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| NL: "Ik begon met een kleine webshop en na een jaar merkte ik dat ik elke dag uren kwijt was aan dezelfde vragen beantwoorden." | "Ik begon een webshop. Klein. Na een jaar was ik elke dag uren kwijt. Aan dezelfde vijf vragen." |
| EN: "I opened the file. I read the report. I saw the number. I called my boss." | "I opened the report. One number. I read it twice — then I called my boss." |

## 9. Tone and energy

**Why.** The viewer copies your energy. If you sound like you'd rather be elsewhere, so will they. Energy also sets
the pace and tells them the topic matters to you.

**How.** Write the delivery into the script: where the energy rises, which word gets the stress, where a short pause
lands (just before the reveal is the classic spot). A naturally calm or soft voice needs a bit more lift than a
punchy or deep one. Energy is not shouting: it is pace, emphasis and conviction. With an AI voice, put the stress and
pauses in the text (short sentences, a dash, a full stop) — they come out in the read.

**Missing when** every line has the same energy note, or the script has no stress or pause marked anywhere.

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| NL: "Dit is een handige tip." (energie: neutraal) | "Deze tip — *één* zin — scheelt je een uur per dag." (energie: hoog op "één", pauze bij de streep) |
| EN: "This changed my business." (flat) | "This *one* habit. It changed everything." (stress on "one", beat of silence after "habit") |

## How they come together: the question chain

A script is a chain of questions. The hook raises the first ("wait, why would that be true?"); each beat answers the
open question and raises the next; the payoff closes the big one; the CTA turns the last open question into an
action ("want the template? comment WORD").

Write the chain before the lines:

| # | Question answered | Question opened | Beat job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | — | Why is the usual way wrong? | hook (contrast) |
| 2 | Why is it wrong? | Then what works? | contrast |
| 3 | What works? | Does it really? | value |
| 4 | Does it really? | How do I start? | proof |
| 5 | How do I start? | What is the last step everyone skips? | tease |
| 6 | What is that step? | Can I get it in full? | payoff |
| 7 | Can I get it? | — | CTA |

Then check every principle against the chain:

- **Curiosity** — every beat opens a question (only the last may close without one).
- **Speed to value** — the first value signal is in beat 1, or beat 2 at the latest, inside 5 seconds.
- **Density** — every beat answers a question someone actually has; a beat that answers nothing goes.
- **Clarity** — each answer fits one or two short sentences; the core point appears twice.
- **Absorption** — at least one beat is an example or picture; the payoff is a step the viewer can take today.
- **Anticipation** — a tease comes before the payoff; the beats build to the best one last.
- **Emotion** — one named emotion runs through the energy column.
- **Rhythm** — sentence lengths vary; most are short.
- **Energy** — the energy column moves (not the same note in every row) and marks where it peaks.

## What script-check measures (and what it can't)

| Principle | Check | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| Curiosity | every beat in the script table has a question answered and opened | error |
| Speed to value | seconds until the `[value]` line (or a heuristic guess) | error if tagged, else warning |
| Density | estimated or measured length against the target | error above +20% |
| Clarity | passive voice, filler words, jargon, long words (NL) or reading grade (EN), the core point said twice | warnings |
| Anticipation | a tease before the payoff | warning |
| Rhythm | share of short sentences, very long sentences, same-length runs, flat variation | warnings |
| Pace | words per second from the voice-over timings | warning |

Contrast strength, emotion, absorption and energy can't be counted. Judge those yourself with the "missing when"
lines above. A clean script-check means nothing is plainly wrong — not that the script is good.

Spoken pace for planning: about 2.8 words per second in Dutch and 3.0 in English for lively reel delivery, so a
30-second reel holds roughly 85 (NL) or 90 (EN) words and a 45-second reel 125 or 135.
